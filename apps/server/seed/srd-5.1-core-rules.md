## Ability Scores and Modifiers

Six abilities describe every creature: Strength, Dexterity, Constitution,
Intelligence, Wisdom and Charisma. Each ability has a score, usually from 3 to
20 for adventurers, and a modifier derived from it. To find a modifier,
subtract 10 from the score and divide by 2, rounding down. A score of 10 or 11
gives +0, 12 or 13 gives +1, 14 or 15 gives +2, 16 or 17 gives +3, 18 or 19
gives +4, and 20 gives +5. A score of 8 or 9 gives -1.

## Proficiency Bonus

Characters have a proficiency bonus set by level: +2 at levels 1 to 4, +3 at
levels 5 to 8, +4 at levels 9 to 12, +5 at levels 13 to 16, and +6 at levels
17 to 20. The bonus is added to attack rolls with weapons and spells the
character is proficient with, to ability checks using proficient skills or
tools, and to proficient saving throws. The bonus is never added to a single
roll more than once.

## Ability Checks

An ability check tests a creature's talent and training against a challenge.
The GM calls for a check when an action has a chance of failure and a
meaningful consequence. Roll a d20 and add the relevant ability modifier, plus
the proficiency bonus if a proficient skill applies. If the total equals or
exceeds the Difficulty Class (DC), the check succeeds.

Typical Difficulty Classes: very easy 5, easy 10, medium 15, hard 20, very
hard 25, nearly impossible 30.

### Contests

When two creatures directly oppose each other, both make ability checks and
compare totals instead of using a DC. The higher total wins. On a tie the
situation stays as it was before the contest.

### Passive Checks

A passive check uses no die roll. The score equals 10 plus all modifiers that
normally apply to the check. Advantage adds 5 and disadvantage subtracts 5.
Passive Wisdom (Perception) is the most common use, for noticing hidden
threats without actively searching.

### Working Together

A character can help another with a task. The character leading the effort
makes the check with advantage. A character can only help if they could
attempt the task alone.

## Advantage and Disadvantage

Sometimes a rule says a roll has advantage or disadvantage. Roll a second d20.
Use the higher of the two rolls with advantage and the lower with
disadvantage. If several circumstances grant advantage, still roll only one
additional d20. If a roll has both advantage and disadvantage, they cancel and
one d20 is rolled, no matter how many circumstances apply on each side.

## Saving Throws

A saving throw is an attempt to resist a spell, trap, poison, disease or
similar threat. Roll a d20 and add the appropriate ability modifier, plus the
proficiency bonus if the creature is proficient in that save. The DC is set by
the effect that caused it. For a spell, the save DC is 8 + the caster's
proficiency bonus + the caster's spellcasting ability modifier.

## Combat

### The Order of Combat

A combat encounter runs in rounds. Each round represents about 6 seconds.

1. Determine surprise. A creature that does not notice a threat is surprised
   and cannot move or take an action on its first turn, and cannot take a
   reaction until that turn ends.
2. Establish positions.
3. Roll initiative. Every participant makes a Dexterity check. The GM ranks
   combatants from the highest total to the lowest; that order is kept for the
   whole encounter.
4. Take turns in initiative order.
5. When everyone has had a turn the round ends and a new round begins.

### Your Turn

On your turn you can move a distance up to your speed and take one action.
You may also take one bonus action if a feature grants one, and you can
interact with one object for free, such as drawing a weapon or opening a door.

### Actions in Combat

- **Attack.** Make one melee or ranged attack.
- **Cast a Spell.** Cast a spell with a casting time of one action.
- **Dash.** Gain extra movement equal to your speed for the turn.
- **Disengage.** Your movement does not provoke opportunity attacks this turn.
- **Dodge.** Until your next turn, attack rolls against you have disadvantage
  if you can see the attacker, and you make Dexterity saves with advantage.
- **Help.** Give an ally advantage on their next ability check or on their
  next attack roll against a creature within 5 feet of you.
- **Hide.** Make a Dexterity (Stealth) check to become hidden.
- **Ready.** Prepare an action to trigger as a reaction on a stated condition.
- **Search.** Make a Wisdom (Perception) or Intelligence (Investigation) check.
- **Use an Object.** Interact with a second object or use an item's feature.

### Making an Attack

Choose a target, determine modifiers, and resolve the attack. To make an
attack roll, roll a d20 and add the appropriate modifiers. If the total equals
or exceeds the target's Armor Class (AC), the attack hits.

Melee weapon attacks use Strength; ranged weapon attacks use Dexterity.
Weapons with the finesse property can use either. Add the proficiency bonus if
the attacker is proficient with the weapon.

**Rolling 1 or 20.** If the d20 shows a 20, the attack hits regardless of
modifiers or AC and is a critical hit. If the d20 shows a 1, the attack misses
regardless of modifiers or AC.

**Critical hits.** On a critical hit, roll all of the attack's damage dice
twice and add them together, then add modifiers as normal.

### Opportunity Attacks

A creature provokes an opportunity attack when it moves out of a hostile
creature's reach. The attacker uses its reaction to make one melee attack
against the provoking creature, interrupting the movement just before the
creature leaves reach. Taking the Disengage action or being moved without
using your own movement, action or reaction does not provoke.

### Grappling

To grapple, use the Attack action to make a special melee attack. The target
must be no more than one size larger than you and within your reach. Instead
of an attack roll, make a Strength (Athletics) check contested by the target's
Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check, the target's choice. If
you win, the target has the grappled condition. You can release the target at
any time, with no action required.

A grappled creature can use its action to escape by succeeding on a Strength
(Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check contested by your Strength
(Athletics) check. When you move, you can drag or carry the grappled creature,
but your speed is halved unless the creature is two or more sizes smaller.

### Shoving

Using the Attack action, you can make a special melee attack to shove a
creature no more than one size larger than you, either knocking it prone or
pushing it 5 feet away. Make a Strength (Athletics) check contested by the
target's Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check.

### Cover

Half cover gives +2 to AC and Dexterity saving throws. Three-quarters cover
gives +5 to AC and Dexterity saving throws. A target with total cover cannot
be targeted directly by an attack or a spell.

### Two-Weapon Fighting

When you take the Attack action with a light melee weapon in one hand, you can
use a bonus action to attack with a different light melee weapon in the other
hand. You do not add your ability modifier to the damage of the bonus attack
unless that modifier is negative.

## Damage and Healing

### Hit Points

Hit points represent a combination of physical and mental durability. A
creature's current hit points can be any number from its hit point maximum
down to 0. Whenever a creature takes damage, that damage is subtracted from
its hit points. Temporary hit points are lost first and are not restored by
healing.

### Resistance and Vulnerability

If a creature has resistance to a damage type, damage of that type is halved.
If it has vulnerability, damage of that type is doubled. Resistance and
vulnerability are applied after all other modifiers.

### Dropping to 0 Hit Points

When damage reduces you to 0 hit points and there is damage remaining, you die
instantly if the remaining damage equals or exceeds your hit point maximum.
Otherwise you fall unconscious.

### Death Saving Throws

Whenever you start your turn with 0 hit points, make a death saving throw:
roll a d20 with no modifiers. On a 10 or higher you succeed; otherwise you
fail. On your third success you become stable. On your third failure you die.
Rolling a 1 counts as two failures. Rolling a 20 means you regain 1 hit point.
If you take damage at 0 hit points you suffer a death saving throw failure, or
two if the damage is from a critical hit.

### Stabilizing a Creature

You can use your action to administer first aid to an unconscious creature and
attempt to stabilize it with a successful DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) check. A
stable creature does not make death saving throws but remains unconscious, and
regains 1 hit point after 1d4 hours.

## Resting

### Short Rest

A short rest is a period of downtime at least 1 hour long. A character can
spend one or more Hit Dice at the end of a short rest, up to their maximum
number of Hit Dice, which equals their level. For each Hit Die spent, roll the
die and add the Constitution modifier; the character regains that many hit
points.

### Long Rest

A long rest is a period of extended downtime at least 8 hours long. At the end
of a long rest a character regains all lost hit points and regains spent Hit
Dice up to half of their total number. A character cannot benefit from more
than one long rest in a 24-hour period and must have at least 1 hit point at
the start of the rest to gain its benefits.

## Conditions

### Blinded

A blinded creature cannot see and automatically fails any ability check that
requires sight. Attack rolls against the creature have advantage, and the
creature's attack rolls have disadvantage.

### Charmed

A charmed creature cannot attack the charmer or target the charmer with
harmful abilities or magical effects. The charmer has advantage on any ability
check to interact socially with the creature.

### Frightened

A frightened creature has disadvantage on ability checks and attack rolls
while the source of its fear is within line of sight. The creature cannot
willingly move closer to the source of its fear.

### Grappled

A grappled creature's speed becomes 0, and it cannot benefit from any bonus to
its speed. The condition ends if the grappler is incapacitated, or if an
effect removes the grappled creature from the reach of the grappler.

### Incapacitated

An incapacitated creature cannot take actions or reactions.

### Invisible

An invisible creature is impossible to see without the aid of magic or a
special sense. Attack rolls against the creature have disadvantage, and the
creature's attack rolls have advantage.

### Poisoned

A poisoned creature has disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks.

### Prone

A prone creature's only movement option is to crawl, unless it stands up,
which costs half its speed. The creature has disadvantage on attack rolls. An
attack roll against the creature has advantage if the attacker is within 5
feet of it; otherwise the attack roll has disadvantage.

### Restrained

A restrained creature's speed becomes 0. Attack rolls against the creature
have advantage, and the creature's attack rolls have disadvantage. The
creature has disadvantage on Dexterity saving throws.

### Stunned

A stunned creature is incapacitated, cannot move, and can speak only
falteringly. It automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws, and
attack rolls against it have advantage.

### Unconscious

An unconscious creature is incapacitated, cannot move or speak, and is unaware
of its surroundings. It drops whatever it is holding and falls prone. It
automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws. Attack rolls against
it have advantage, and any attack that hits is a critical hit if the attacker
is within 5 feet.

## Adventuring

### Falling

At the end of a fall, a creature takes 1d6 bludgeoning damage for every 10
feet it fell, to a maximum of 20d6, and lands prone unless it avoids taking
damage from the fall.

### Vision and Light

In a lightly obscured area such as dim light, creatures have disadvantage on
Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on sight. A heavily obscured area such as
darkness blocks vision entirely; a creature there is effectively blinded.

### Travel Pace

A party can travel at a fast pace (4 miles per hour, -5 to passive Perception),
a normal pace (3 miles per hour), or a slow pace (2 miles per hour, able to
use stealth).
